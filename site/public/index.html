A screen for the machines you love.

Machinedrum Editor and Monomachine Editor are desktop editors for the Elektron Machinedrum and Monomachine. Each job gets one screen: a step grid, drawn parameter locks, a kit library, a piano roll. Underneath, the machine's real firmware runs in joelanders' emulator, or the editor drives your own machine over MIDI.

01 · The real firmware

The machine's own OS

The emulator runs Elektron's operating system (Machinedrum OS 1.63, Monomachine OS 1.32B), so the sound engine and the sequencer are the machine's own. The editor reads the playhead, mutes and chains from the machine's memory.

02 · Screen-native

One screen per job

Not a picture of the front panel. Six workspaces per editor, each laid out for one task, every value visible and editable. Keys 1–6 switch between them; on the Machinedrum, ? lists every shortcut.

03 · Or your machine Beta

Emulator or hardware

Switch the engine to HW MIDI and the same editor drives a real Machinedrum or Monomachine over MIDI. This mode has only been tested against the emulator so far, not on real hardware.

Features

What is in the box

Only what works today. Where something works only with the emulator, it says so.

One-screen workspaces MD · MM

Sequence, Sound, Mix, Song and Control on both, plus Sampler (MD) and Perform (MM). Switch with keys 1–6. On the MD, ? shows all 43 shortcuts.

The step grid MD · MM

Every track on one grid, 16 steps a page or all 64 at once. Trigs, accents and slides with a click and a modifier.

Drawn lock lanes MD · MM

Pick a parameter and draw its locks under the steps. Pan-like values get a centred, bipolar lane.

Kit and pattern library MD · MM

Read from the machine, then load, save as, copy, paste, clear, rename or drag-copy slots. Library writes share one undo history with your edits.

Chains and mutes MD · MM

Mutes live on the track keys, and the machine's own mutes are read back. On the MD, Shift prepares several at once, and chains are the firmware's own, read from its memory.

Note sequencer and piano roll MM

Notes with length, slide and swing in a piano roll per track. Grid and live recording, with the recorded notes read back as you play (emulator).

The routing view MD · MM

The MM draws how tracks sum into its buses in each routing mode. The MD shows the send path and which tracks skip it on outputs A–F.

App LFOs MD · MM

LFO and random sources that the app runs and sends as CCs, within 300 per second. In the Machinedrum plug-in they keep running with the editor window closed.

MIDI learn and the control matrix MD · MM

Press L and move a knob. The matrix shows every source against every track, each target with its own range and curve.

Hardware-true plates MD · MM

The Machinedrum Editor wears the silver MKI or the black MKII plate. The Monomachine keeps one plate, and MKI mode tells you which machines need a MKII.

Emulator or your machine Beta

Run the real firmware in the emulator, or switch to HW MIDI and edit a real machine at MIDI DIN speed. Not tested on real hardware yet.

Plug-in and standalone MD · MM

VST3 and AU for your DAW, and a standalone app with its own audio and MIDI panel. On the MD, app modulators and knob CCs are saved with your project.

Two engines

Emulator, or the one on your desk

By default each editor runs its machine's own operating system inside joelanders' emulator. That is the most complete mode: the editor can read the playhead, the working kit, mutes and chains straight from the machine's memory, and the start-up screen is the firmware's own.

HW MIDI Beta swaps the emulator for your own Machinedrum or Monomachine on the plug-in's MIDI in and out. The editor sends at DIN speed (3,125 bytes a second) and reads back what it changes. It has been tested only with the emulator standing in as the machine. If you try it on a real one, please tell us how it went.

What HW MIDI cannot do

The machines have no MIDI message for these, so the editor says so rather than pretending:

  • Sample transfers (MD)No sample sending (SDS), sample names, memory in use, or RAM to ROM over MIDI.
  • Memory-only viewsLive recording, chaining, reading the working kit and the start-up screen need the emulator.
  • MonomachineMIDI-track mutes, record modes, playhead and tempo are not reported over MIDI. Pattern, song and global dumps wait until you open SYSEX RECV on the machine; the editor tells you how.
  • CLEARThere is no clear command, so the editor writes an empty kit or pattern instead.
Honest limits

What to expect

  • Alpha software.Both editors are new. Keep backups of your projects and of your machine's data.
  • macOS only for now.Built and tested on Apple silicon (an M4 Pro). Other platforms are not tested.
  • Not signed by Apple.The first launch needs right-click → Open. The download page explains it.
  • About half a CPU core per instance.On an M4 Pro the emulation takes 44–48% of one core; the standalone with the editor open, about 55%. One audio thread carries two instances at most. Dropouts in a DAW have not been measured yet.
  • A few things are unverified.On the MD, local control and TRIG IN A/B are stored and shown but their effect is not confirmed. On the MM, step recording is not in the editor yet.
Bring your own ROM

No firmware is included

The editors run Elektron's operating system, which is Elektron's copyrighted software. It is not included, we cannot supply it, and please don't ask for it. These editors are for people who own the instruments.

  • MachinedrumOS 1.63 firmware image, 8 MiB. MKI, MKII and UW units use the same image.
  • MonomachineOS 1.32B firmware image, 8 MiB.

The editor checks the image's fingerprint and keeps it on your computer only.

System requirements
  • Mac with Apple siliconTested on an Apple M4 Pro with macOS 27.
  • A VST3 or AU hostFor the plug-in. The standalone app needs nothing else.
  • Your own firmware imageSee above.
  • OptionalA Machinedrum or Monomachine and a MIDI interface, for HW MIDI (beta).
